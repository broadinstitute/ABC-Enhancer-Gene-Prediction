Summary

  • Every H3K27ac counting variant beats accessibility alone, but the margin is far bigger for ATAC (AUPRC 0.457 -> 0.52-0.54, Fig. 1b) than for DNase (0.576 -> 0.58-0.60, Fig. 1a) — H3K27ac adds much less new information on top of DNase than on top of ATAC in this benchmark.
  • Within the H3K27ac variants, flank-only and element+-flank counting both edge out plain element counting (best DNase AUPRC 0.601 vs. 0.579; best ATAC AUPRC 0.537 vs. 0.519, Fig. 1), but every variant’s 95% CI overlaps every other variant’s — none of these pairwise differences is resolved by this benchmark’s sample size.
  • Flank width (150 vs. 200 vs. 500 bp) makes almost no difference on its own: within either counting family, AUPRC changes by <=0.01 across the three widths (Fig. 1), well inside the CI width of ~0.05.
  • The flank-counting advantage over element-only counting is concentrated at 10-100 kb enhancer-gene distance for both assays (Fig. 3); at <10 kb it nearly vanishes, and at >100 kb all variants converge to low AUPRC with wide, overlapping CIs from small positive counts (n = 82).

Goals

Test whether counting H3K27ac ChIP-seq reads over a symmetric flank around each candidate element (element +/-N bp), or over the flanks alone (excluding the element), changes ABC prediction accuracy relative to the pipeline’s current element-only counting — and whether the answer depends on flank width (150/200/500 bp) or on which accessibility assay (DNase vs. ATAC) defines the candidate elements.

Flank-based counting nudges AUPRC up for both assays, but not past the noise floor

Q: Does extending H3K27ac counting into the flanks (or restricting it to the flanks) change AUPRC against the CRISPR benchmark, for all element-gene pairs?

A: Every flank-based variant scores numerically above plain element counting for both assays (best DNase AUPRC 0.601 vs. 0.579; best ATAC AUPRC 0.537 vs. 0.519), but the 95% CIs of all eight variants overlap heavily within each assay (Fig. 1).

Method.

  • ABC predictions run once per counting variant (12-16 K562 biosample combinations: 2 assays x {accessibility-only, element, element+-flank at 150/200/500 bp, flank-only at 150/200/500 bp}), qnorm off.
  • Benchmarked against EPCrisprBenchmark_ensemble_data_GRCh38.tsv.gz via the CRISPR_comparison pipeline (comparePredictionsToExperiment.Rmd), split into separate DNase-only and ATAC-only comparisons.
  • AUPRC and its 95% CI come directly from performance_summary.txt in each comparison’s results directory.

The precision gain at fixed sensitivity is largest for element+-500bp on ATAC, flank-only-500bp on DNase

Q: At a fixed 70% sensitivity, does precision (fraction of predicted pairs that are true positives) follow the same ranking as AUPRC?

A: Mostly yes, but not exactly: DNase’s best precision is flank-only 500 bp (0.507 vs. 0.469 for element), while ATAC’s best precision is element +/-500 bp (0.444 vs. 0.402 for element) — the AUPRC-best ATAC variant (flank-only 150 bp, precision 0.401) is not the precision-best one.

The flank-counting advantage is concentrated at 10-100kb enhancer-gene distance

Q: Does the small AUPRC advantage of flank-based counting hold at every enhancer-gene distance, or is it distance-dependent?

A: The gap between element-only and the best flank variant is small at <10 kb (DNase 0.790 vs. 0.818 AUPRC) and widens in relative terms at 10-100 kb (DNase 0.543 vs. 0.575; ATAC 0.499 vs. 0.504), before all variants converge to low AUPRC with wide, overlapping CIs beyond 100 kb (n = 82 positives).

Open questions / next steps

  • None of the element-vs-flank or flank-width comparisons clears its own 95% CI — is a larger CRISPR benchmark (more E-G pairs, or a cross-dataset combination) needed to resolve whether flank-based counting is a real improvement, or is the true effect simply this small?
  • DNase and ATAC disagree on which flank family wins on precision (flank-only for DNase, element+-flank for ATAC) — worth checking whether this is noise or reflects a real difference in how the two assays’ candidate regions interact with flanking H3K27ac signal.
  • The 100 kb-2.5 Mb bin has only 82 positives across both assays; any distance-dependence claim there should be treated as exploratory only.
  • [Q for Jesse/lab] Given the gains are within noise, is it worth adopting flank-based H3K27ac counting as a pipeline default, or keeping element-only until a larger benchmark can resolve the comparison?

Methods

Quantile normalization (use_qnorm) was off for every run in this comparison. Qnorm exists to make signal comparable across cell types by mapping each sample’s distribution onto a K562 reference; since every biosample here already is K562, and that reference was itself built assuming element-only counting, applying it here would have confounded the comparison between counting geometries. alpha (the plotted score cutoff) was correspondingly left blank for all H3K27ac-variant predictors in pred_config_h3k27ac_counting_{DHS,ATAC}.tsv — the pre-calibrated abc_thresholds.tsv values assume qnorm’d data and would be misleading here. All comparisons therefore rest on the continuous ABC.Score (AUPRC, precision-recall), not any binary threshold.
